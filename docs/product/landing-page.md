## 1. Positioning — the application intelligence layer

The earlier "two versions, sequenced" plan (assistant-first hero now, interface-layer hero when Phase 6 ships) was **superseded 2026-08-13** by the founder's brief: the homepage claims the category **now**.

- **The claim:** *the Application Intelligence Layer of your SaaS.* FlowBuddy learns how the software works and turns that knowledge into an intelligence layer for its users and AI agents. The reaction the page is built for is "I haven't seen this category before" — explicitly not "another Intercom / Appcues / AI chatbot." So the page must never LEAD with chat widgets, tours, or support automation; those appear later, as outcomes of the layer. Sophistication is revealed progressively: claim → proof → use cases → mechanics → outcomes.
- **Hero copy (locked, founder's brief — H1 amended by the founder 2026-08-14):** no eyebrow · H1 "Application Intelligence Layer / for your SaaS" (two lines; line one larger in a static brand gradient — no shimmer — with the twinkling spark; line two smaller in ink) · sub "FlowBuddy learns how your software works and turns that knowledge into an intelligence layer for your users and AI agents."
- **The claim is proven by a transformation, not asserted** (three beats, founder decision 2026-08-14): record your product → the application intelligence layer (the workflow knowledge base) is built from the recording → one trunk fans out to the four products it powers. The hero draws that flow; the layer section then pins the intelligence card while the uses scroll past it — the same visual, deliberately, so the thing built in the hero is the thing pinned below.
- **Two agent claims, kept distinct on every surface.** FlowBuddy's own **in-product agent execution** is sold as **Beta**, and always with the full trust framing: off by default, switched on per workspace, every run starts with the user's explicit consent, narrated, stoppable, outcome-verified. **Third-party agent access** stays *direction*, never available — the rule is the phase's own ([`interop.md`](../build/interop.md) states it; the roadmap says whether it still binds). `/future` no longer argues the category (the homepage absorbed that); it argues only the road ahead, with the beta rung shown between "live" and "in development."
- **Scope of address:** "your SaaS" — the buyer is unchanged.

## 2. Page structure (as built)

1. **Hero** — the claim (centered) + a full-width animated **flow diagram in three beats**: the recording (a mini product form the cursor actually works through — two typed inputs and two clicks, REC pill) → the layer being built from it (the glowing indigo core — "Application Intelligence Layer / Your workflow knowledge base" — on a dot-grid ground, where **each captured action flies in as a chip and stacks as a numbered step** of the workflow, then "✓ Approved by you") → a one-trunk-four-branch **fan-out** whose branches carry flowing current and light each product in turn; the four tiles are **real links** to the capability pages (agent tile carries the Beta pill). The full story **replays on a continuous loop** (founder call 2026-08-15), with the dash current, core float and sheen running throughout; each beat carries its numbered caption above the visual. Design-system rules unchanged: product recreated in HTML on the tokens, never a fake photo; the markup ships the fully-lit end state so no-JS / reduced-motion / crawlers get the complete diagram, and all continuous motion is reduced-motion-gated. The old chat-widget hero demo retired — its job is done by the assistant mock in the layer section and, verbatim, by the assistant capability page's hero.
2. **What it is** — the layer-in-your-stack explainer (the paper band between the white hero and the tinted centrepiece), a close replication of the founder's reference image on the tokens (design-system rule: recreated in HTML, never an image): the familiar SaaS stack — Data → API → UI — as isometric slabs with icon + caption **on the slab plane**, and the **Intelligence Layer lifted above the stack as a flat horizontal card** — indigo glass ("Understands. Guides. Acts.", NEW LAYER badge, five capability tiles: Understand Workflows · Provide Guidance · Deliver Support · Drive Analytics · AI Agents), with pulsing light beams dropping into the UI slab. Rise-in reveal only; finished state served.
3. **One intelligence layer** — the centrepiece scroll sequence, recast from "one knowledge base." The pinned indigo card IS the layer (workflows · concepts · screens, "approved by you"), and **seven** consumers arrive along the rail: **four live** — In-App AI Assistant · Contextual Guidance · Interactive Onboarding · **AI Agent Execution** (brand-tinted **Beta** pill; its mock plays the consented run: approval → narrated steps → "Done — outcome verified") — and three unbuilt (Autogenerated Help Portal · SOP Generation · Third-Party Agent Access) in **greyscale** with a muted "Coming soon" pill, so no picture can read as a shipped screen. The section keeps its `#knowledge-base` anchor (nav, footer and the "See it in action" fallback aim at it); the nav label is now "Use cases." Scroll/pin mechanics and their tuning numbers are unchanged and owned by the source comments.
4. **How it works** — Record → Approve → Go live, now **below** the use cases: the brief inverts the objection order (prove the category first; clear the setup-cost objection after). Copy speaks the layer vocabulary ("converts your walkthrough into structured application intelligence"). Sections still alternate white and paper with the band belonging to the **position** — the reorder moved the paper band off this section (it is now the white band between the tinted centrepiece and paper Benefits).
5. **Benefits** — the four outcome tiles, unchanged.
6. **Sign-up block** — copy is provisional; the final content is an open decision.
7. **FAQ** — objection-shaped Q&As, mirrored into `FAQPage` structured data. It now **leads with the category question** ("What is an application intelligence layer?" — the AEO land-grab: nobody owns that query yet) and carries the beta acting answer plus the two-claims agent answer linking `/future`.
8. **Footer** (+ `/future` under "The road ahead").

**Top nav carries only the two section links** (use cases · how it works), in page order.

**Capability pages — four dedicated pages, one per live consumer** (2026-08-14): `/in-app-ai-assistant` · `/contextual-guidance` · `/interactive-onboarding` · `/ai-agent-execution`. Shared anatomy, capability-first then layer-story: a hero in the old homepage-hero layout (text left, looping HTML product demo right) → capability-specific how-it-works steps → a checklist detail band → a "powered by the application intelligence layer" band that mirrors the homepage's pinned card and cross-links the sibling pages → the sign-up block → a page-scoped FAQ with its own `FAQPage` JSON-LD. Two decisions worth keeping: the **assistant page's hero is the retired assistant-first homepage hero, reused verbatim** — it is the assistant answering in context, which is that page's whole claim; and the **agent page sits inside the beta guardrail end to end** (Beta pill beside the hero eyebrow, consent/stop/verify copy, caption "In beta · Off by default · Every run needs your user's consent"). The homepage's four live tiles link out ("Explore …" under each mock), the footer's Product column lists all four, and the pages join `sitemap.xml` and `llms.txt`'s page list. The slugs live in one registry the tiles, footer and sibling links all read, so a renamed page moves everywhere at once.

**`/faq` — the full question set.** Same register rules as before, with rule 2 amended: acting is sold as **beta** with the full trust framing; what stays direction-only is **third-party** agent access. It opens with the FlowBuddy-AI-phrased identity answers ("What is FlowBuddy AI?" now leads with the layer; "What is an application intelligence layer?" is the definitional AEO entry).

**Deliberately out:** fake logos/testimonials · a pricing table ("free during early access" instead) · a standalone "what FlowBuddy is" prose section — the pinned layer card carries the definition visually, which is why no prose section returned with the repositioning.

**The demo is the page itself:** the real widget embeds as the landing page's own overlay (FlowBuddy answering questions about FlowBuddy) — no demo section. The embed renders only when the build provides the landing widget key (names & defaults: `.env.example`), so the page never blocks on the dogfood KB existing.

**SEO/GEO on-page:** canonical + OG/Twitter meta (image alts included) with a branded `og.png` card rendered from the design tokens, JSON-LD (`Organization` + `SoftwareApplication` site-wide, `FAQPage` on every page carrying Q&As, `BreadcrumbList` on every subpage), `robots.txt` (explicit allow-groups for the named AI/LLM crawlers), `llms.txt` (the declarative product summary for generative engines), `sitemap.xml` with lastmod, a noindexed 404 page, declarative meta descriptions.

**The machine-readable surfaces sit inside §1's copy guardrail too, and are the easiest place to breach it.** `SoftwareApplication.featureList` and `llms.txt` are read as claims of what exists, by engines that will repeat them without the page's hedging around them. `featureList` names the four live capabilities with agent execution explicitly "(beta)" and its consent framing inline; `llms.txt` states the beta conditions (off by default, consented, narrated, stoppable, verified) and names the three unbuilt consumers as in development and NOT yet available. The pull is real: the tempting summary flattens beta into shipped and in-development into real.

## 3. Open items

1. **Sign-up block (section 5) final copy** — placeholder-quality copy shipped; the founder decides the real content.
2. **Live-widget dogfood prerequisites:** record + approve a FlowBuddy-on-FlowBuddy KB in Studio, allowlist `flowbuddyai.com`, and set the widget key on the Render landing service.
3. **The brand name is half-migrated.** The visible wordmarks (header, footer), `/faq`, and now the homepage `<title>` say **"FlowBuddy AI"**; the identity strings say **"FlowBuddy"** — `Organization.name`, `SoftwareApplication.name`, `og:site_name`, the `© 2026` line, and `llms.txt` throughout. Those are what search engines, link previews and generative engines treat as the entity's name, so the split is worth resolving deliberately rather than one surface at a time. The copyright line is a separate question again: it names a legal entity, not a wordmark.
4. **Off-page SEO/GEO** (content, articles, listings, Search Console/Bing submission) — untouched; a separate work stream.
